# Characters

This document is the official place to maintain character scope, data boundaries, asset sources and the progress of the character phase. For general features see [Features](FEATURES.md), and for hands-on operation see the [manual test checklist](../SELF_TEST.md).

## Adopted character direction (2026-09-07)

Feibi Jiubi does not eat or lick icons, has no hunger bar, and uses a separate mischief bar above her head. The code isolates character capabilities through `SupportsFood` / `UsesMischief`: Feibi does not start the deletion and icon-consumption watchers, does not join the eating queue, does not choose hunger behaviors, and keeps no food lines or food animation entry points; Nuonuo keeps the existing eating gameplay. The Fasting Mode switch still belongs to Nuonuo and cannot replace character isolation.

The adopted gameplay is that she normally kicks, punches, charges and stashes windows in her hat, and with a full bar throws her hat and performs a shattering super kick or a two-handed tear, then picks up her hat, stashes the shards or two halves of the real snapshot in the hat opening, and puts her hat on to return to daily life; the window is kept until a right-click restore. For the complete design and safety boundaries, see the [overall window mischief plan](FEIBI_WINDOW_PLAY_PLAN.md). Wiring in the code does not mean the assets and desktop effect have been accepted; the last section of this page maintains the current progress centrally. Flattening into a pancake, paper planes and crumple-and-shoot remain later candidates.

## Adopted scope and switching rules

Nuonuo or Feibi Jiubi is chosen inside the existing WPF character window, reusing physics, terrain, dragging, sleep and general settings, with optional both-pets coexistence and interactions between the characters. Mischief snapshots and the hat use transparent effect windows, and window results are kept according to the rules in the next section; autonomously chasing, dodging, following or hugging the mouse stays removed. Switching characters does not restart the process.

The tray "Pet Character" lists both characters. The check mark, tooltip name, size label and last choice are only updated after a successful load; `SetCharacter` only syncs the display. Switch requests during dragging, Nuonuo's swallowing or the current satisfied animation are held and applied at a safe boundary; clicking the currently checked character again cancels a pending switch. Switching to Feibi clears the old food queue and fake icons, without handing pending meals to Feibi or adding meal counts; Nuonuo's completed eating records are kept. A switch requested while Feibi is making mischief first cancels the interaction and tries to return any window still stashed by the pet.

Before switching, the old character's progress data is saved, and the new character's animations, voice service and save data are prepared; if frames are missing or preparation fails, the old character is kept. At startup, if the last chosen non-default character cannot be loaded, it falls back to Nuonuo. If a non-default character's tray icon is missing or unreadable, the system application icon is used, never Nuonuo's character icon.

## Characters and data boundaries

### Window results restored by the user (Adopted)

After the hat stash, shatter kick and tear performances finish, the real window stays minimized. After a special move's attack, she first picks up her hat and holds it steady, then uses the existing `hat_store` original frames to stash the shards or two halves into the hat opening one by one within 3 seconds, and puts the hat on to return to daily life; once collected, no window shards or leftover cards remain on the desktop. There is no countdown return, and the whole window is not restored automatically. Only choosing "Give the Windows Back" by right-clicking the character or the tray takes it out: the stashed destruction result first comes out of the current hat opening, then reassembles at the original position, and the hat goes back on once the real window is confirmed restored. Clicking the hat or the shards does not trigger a return; the effects while holding let the mouse click through and never block desktop operation.

`PrankEffectWindow` saves the final drawing transform of each real snapshot piece; stashing only adds an arc toward the hat opening, shrinking and brim clipping, without replacing the shards with a whole card. `SetCollectionProgress` is separate from the original destruction progress, and `IsCollected` does not mean the system has restored the window; after reverse retrieval, the pieces still reassemble through the original `Shatter` / `Tear` type and the original snapshot. This local path only serves the two existing special moves, is maintained by the project maintainer, and adds no assets or general animation framework.

The tear first stretches the whole snapshot vertically, then splits it into upper and lower whole pieces along the same horizontal jagged break line, without a sideways sweeping crop. There is no crack before the break; after it, the grip points of the two pieces follow the upper and lower hands respectively, and after she lets go they freeze at the maximum spread position. The real current character frame of `bare_tear` is drawn in front of the snapshot while the original character image is hidden, avoiding a covered face or a translucent double image; the hand anchors and the foreground both use the actual screen transform of the character visual, including the temporary size and squash/stretch. Hat actions keep using the verified hat-opening foreground mask. The existing sprite frames and anchors are unchanged, and no images are regenerated or edited.

The tear's dedicated temporary size reuses the existing WPF size and body squash/stretch: after throwing the hat she grows over 0.8 seconds; the target considers both the user's size and the window height, is normally about 2.25–3 times her original size, and is limited to 70% of the work area's short side; a character who is already very big does not shrink for the performance. `Core/TearPrankPresentation.cs` maintains this local size and pose rule, and `MainWindow.Tear.cs` handles foot anchoring, on-screen positioning, growing and shrinking back. It does not call `ChangePetSize`, which saves preferences, and does not change the user's size setting; after letting go on frame 10 she shrinks back smoothly, and completion, interruption, character switching and display changes all clean up the temporary size. This path only serves the existing tear, is maintained by the project maintainer, and no general transformation system is extracted.

### Single-slot window stash and parallel small actions

The hat holds at most one window result, but an existing stash no longer stops her from kicking, punching or charging another compatible window; the "Make Mischief Now" menu item stays available, and while performing, the character state still prevents re-entry. While the hat is occupied, she no longer hides a second window inside it, and does not start a new shatter or tear that needs to stash into the hat; a full bar stays ready, and small mischief continues as usual. Ordinary idling, dragging, going home, resizing, Quiet Mode, turning off autonomous mischief and switching to other apps all keep the stash, never overwriting it or returning it automatically.

`MainWindow.PrankStorage.cs` uses a lazy single slot: when a performance finishes, the original `WindowPrankController` keeps holding its own window and in-memory snapshot; only when trying to start the next kick, punch or charge is that complete controller, together with its target, snapshot, action type, contact point and hat-opening state, parked in the stash slot, and a separate controller created for the current small action. It reuses the existing ownership checks, `IsHeld` / `HoldSnapshot()` and the native return evidence, without copying the snapshot or handing the old result to the controller that is moving another window. An ordinary small action being rejected, cancelled or failing only ends that action and never returns the window in the hat because of it; the stash keeps undergoing its own safety checks.

When the user chooses "Give the Windows Back", the current small action is cancelled first, then the stash's original controller, original snapshot, original action type and contact point are taken back, and the same window is returned along the original whole-card or shards/two-halves path. `WindowPrankHoldPolicy` maintains the rule for whether an interruption must return the stash. This approach only serves Feibi's "one stashed result + one current small action", is maintained by the project maintainer, adds no multi-slot inventory framework, and never persists window handles or screenshots. `PrankStorageParked` / `PrankStorageReturnRequested` record parking and retrieval, and cannot replace real window acceptance.

Safe exit, switching characters, screen lock/sleep, and real display layout or work-area changes still clean up the current small action and try to return the stash; if the stash's own identity or safety check fails, it is handled by the original ownership rules. When the user restores, closes or takes over the stashed target from the taskbar, its ownership is released immediately without minimizing it again; operating on another small-action target does not count as taking over the stash. Ordinary pet screen-crossing or DPI adaptation does not trigger a return. A crash cannot guarantee an automatic return, but the window itself is not closed and can still be restored from the taskbar. Snapshots stay only in memory, never written to disk or uploaded; the status log records the handle, phase, whether it is held and the return result, never the window content. `prank.snapshot.held` / `PrankCompleted.AwaitingManualRestore` mark the waiting state, `PrankRemainsStored` marks that the shards have been stashed, and `PrankManualRestoreCompleted` marks that the user's return is complete.

The character IDs, runtime folders and rhythm parameters have `Core/PetCharacterProfile.cs` as their code entry point; at this stage there are only these two real consumers, using immutable configuration and the existing state machine, with no separate plugin protocol or general character framework. The project maintainer is responsible for keeping the configuration, action contract, voice mapping and this page's records in sync.

### Both-pets coexistence and synced settings (Adopted 2026-09-23, awaiting hands-on acceptance)

The official everyday launcher adds a persistent "**Both Pets Together**" switch. When enabled, the current character keeps her original window, and the other character appears in a separate transparent window near the current character on the same monitor; the two windows run their original animations, physics, needs and character-specific features separately in the same process, sharing one tray/right-click menu and the same settings. Turning coexistence off ends the companion character and saves her original separate save data; choosing any character from "Pet Character" also switches back to single-pet mode. The release build's single-instance lock and the separate launchers of the exclusive test edition and the Behavior Control Edition are unchanged. The implementation is in `MainWindow.Coexistence.cs`, `MainWindow.xaml.cs` and `TrayIconService.cs`, only serves the existing two characters, and is maintained by the project maintainer.

The size slider, mute, Quiet Mode, "Bring Her Home" and Infinite Mode apply to both at once. Fasting Mode only changes Nuonuo's hunger logic; autonomous mischief and returning windows are only done by Feibi, but the coexistence menu shows both sets of exclusive entries. Infinite Mode is a new persistent option: both above-head hunger/mischief bars are hidden, Nuonuo's hunger is fixed at zero and she stops begging for food, licking icons and rolling from hunger; Feibi's mischief charge, cooldown and window safety conditions still follow the original rules. Turning Infinite Mode off restores the previously, separately set Fasting Mode, without resetting Feibi's mischief value. The characters still save their life and mischief states separately, without merging save data; for two-person actions and the icon relay see [Two-person interactions](INTERACTION_PLAN.md).

Progress bar sync rule adopted 2026-09-28: while together, if either Fasting Mode or Infinite Mode is on, both characters' progress bars are hidden; they only show again together when both are off. After leaving coexistence, Nuonuo alone still follows Fasting/Infinite Mode, and Feibi alone only has her mischief bar hidden by Infinite Mode, unaffected by the kept Fasting Mode preference. This reuses the existing shared settings and `PetModePolicy`, with no separate switch or save migration; it only syncs visibility, without merging values or changing Feibi's charge and cooldown. After the menu writes the shared settings, both are refreshed immediately, and turning coexistence on/off and switching characters also recalculate; the refresh does not call the mischief simulation, so toggling progress bars does not trigger extra actions. This rule and the `ProgressDisplayVisibilityChanged` diagnostic are maintained by this project; automated regression covers mode combinations, switching order and restarts with either main character; the desktop effect is accepted manually by the user.

### Right-click menu interaction pause (Adopted 2026-09-08)

The character and the tray reuse the same menu. `TrayIconService.MenuInteractionStrip` notifies `IsMenuOpen` / `MenuOpenChanged` synchronously when the root menu is Opening; while it is open, the character simulation, frame switching, walking and terrain scanning do not advance, while the window ownership safety check every 200 ms is kept. Closing, cancelling the opening or releasing the menu all reset it; switching to the character submenu does not resume walking, and repeated right-clicks do not Show repeatedly. `MainWindow.Menu.cs` resets the frame clock and platform motion sampling on close, so catch-up frames do not fast-forward and window displacement during the menu is not mistaken for a throwing force. It does not modify character state, user settings or save data, and menu commands still run.

`WindowPrankController.CheckSafety()` is separated from motion advancement, and the foreground HWND of the pet's own menu is not treated as switching to an external app; when the real target is taken over, closed, restored or a safety exception occurs, the existing rules still apply. New `PetMenuOpened` / `PetMenuClosed` logs record the action and how long the menu was open, and `UiFrameSlow` records UI frame work over 100 ms, the state before and after switching, and the number of loaded assets, at most one entry per 5 seconds, never window content. This choice reuses the menu and animation cache and only isolates interaction and continuous movement, without migrating the UI framework; the project maintainer is responsible for this lifecycle. The existing logs cannot prove a single root cause, and whether the menu responds better on real hardware still needs the user to retest.

Nuonuo keeps hunger, fullness, sleepiness, curiosity and eating statistics. Feibi reuses sleepiness, curiosity and their limited offline evolution; hunger and fullness in old saves are reset to zero on load and at runtime, historical food fields take no part in behavior and no longer grow. The mischief state separately saves its value and remaining cooldown, never window handles, snapshots or pending actions; no mischief builds up offline. Quiet Mode only allows autonomous idling and sleeping, stops performances but keeps windows already confirmed stashed, and restores them by the window holding rules.

### Autonomous mischief defaults and rhythm

The user has approved autonomous mischief being on by default, applying to existing installations as well. `PetSettings` defaults to `Version=5`, `AllowMischief=true`; `SettingsStore.Load()` updates configurations with a version below 5 to on once and saves them, while other existing preferences are kept according to their own migration rules. If the user turns it off in the menu after the upgrade, loading v5 and later never turns it on again. Mute, character choice and size are not changed by this v5 migration; `FeatureDemoSession` still only reads the real display preferences, explicitly turns autonomous mischief off in separate temporary v5 settings, and never triggers or saves a real configuration migration.

`Core/MischiefController.cs` is the code entry point for the rhythm parameters, and this section is the official place to maintain the corresponding product rules:

| Item | Adopted rule |
| --- | --- |
| Autonomous bar filling | Going from 0 to 100 takes 300 seconds of effective active time; mischief performances, sleep, Quiet Mode, screen lock, menu pauses and offline time do not count as effective filling time; walking and dragging can still charge it, and while being dragged she waits for the user to let go before considering a strike |
| Ordinary autonomous mischief | Tries once after a random 60–120 seconds; after finishing, a new interval is drawn, and 6 mischief points are no longer deducted |
| Special move | A full bar produces only one intent; after finishing, the bar resets to zero and cools down for 120 seconds, with no repeated re-triggering |
| No suitable target | Waits 10 seconds before checking again; a full bar stays at 100, without dropping because there is no window and without widening the target safety range |
| Hat holds something | No second window stash or special move is triggered; a full bar keeps waiting for the hat to empty, and she can still kick, punch and charge other windows at the ordinary rhythm |
| Teasing and turning autonomy off | Each effective tease adds 12 points, at least 1.5 seconds apart; after autonomy is turned off, manual teasing and mischief still work, and no automatic actions are queued |
| Genuine interruption | Distinguished from waiting with no target; interrupted performances still keep the protection of at most 85 points and a cooldown of at least 90 seconds, and pauses/offline time store no burst intent |

These are effective times and attempt intervals, not a guarantee of happening to find a safe target at wall-clock times. The bar does not fill during the special-move cooldown; small mischief the user triggers manually can still run, without bypassing the special-move cooldown. Sleep, Quiet Mode, screen lock and render gaps over 5 seconds build up no bursts; a normal restart only loads the value and remaining cooldown, without restoring old pending events.

Feibi's menu keeps the "Allow Autonomous Mischief" switch and adds an unclickable "Mischief status" line explaining off, charging, cooldown, waiting for a suitable window, an occupied hat or the current reason for pausing; while being dragged it shows "Charging · waiting for you to let go", and values below full are rounded down, so 99.7 is not shown as 100. When off, the above-head bar turns gray and does not pulse; when turned back on it uses blue-purple, without announcing the bar value in a bubble. `MainWindow.MischiefStatus.cs` chooses the status text, `TrayIconService.SetMischiefStatus` only updates display text that changed, and the original menu pause lifecycle and size slider are kept. The `MischiefStatus` log records the reason, bar value, cooldown and next attempt time on state changes and with rate limiting, never target content. This status display and the parameter adjustments only apply to Feibi and are maintained by the project maintainer; how often it interferes on real hardware still needs the user to retest.

## Personality and voice

Feibi Jiubi keeps the original image's big white hat, light-blonde long hair, purple eyes, blue/black/white outfit and two little feet. Her liveliness comes from actively pushing off the ground, bouncing on landing, the inertia of her hat brim and hair, and small reactions that fit the scene.

- Movement speed is `1.15` times Nuonuo's base value; the maximum standing idle time is 4 seconds, while Nuonuo stays at 5 seconds.
- When full of energy she leans more toward exploring and jumping; when sleepy, movement weights drop, and she still sleeps. No support, action cooldowns and life-mode limits take priority over personality.
- Idling, walking, running, curiosity, climbing, jumping and landing use selective rhythm adjustments, sleep keeps a readable duration; mischief sets wind-up and rebound times action by action, without speeding everything up globally.
- Both characters share the optional speech-bubble switch, off by default; when on, it reuses each character's own line catalog to show short status responses, and when both pets are idle they take turns talking. Test hints and mischief bar values still stay in their original menu/bar spots and never go into bubbles. Details follow [Two-pet interactions](INTERACTION_PLAN.md#speech-bubbles-above-the-head).
- While standing idle, Feibi tries to play one of her own everyday recordings every 20–50 seconds, subject to mute, Quiet Mode, cooldowns and per-recording anti-repeat limits; switching stops the previous character's sound. Nuonuo does not get this everyday voice timer.
- Both characters use their own dedicated recordings, `HasVoice=true`; switching characters first stops and releases the old player, then uses the new character's folder and mapping. Mute and volume settings are shared; neither character borrows the other's recordings, synthesized voices or notification sounds.

### Feibi's dedicated voice

The 25 WAV files the user provided were copied as-is into `assets/characters/feibijiubi/audio`, without modifying the source files. The user confirmed that all 9 numbered recordings are used for everyday random talking; the emotion mapping follows the file names the user provided, the content was not listened to or transcribed, and the actual sound and fit to scenes await manual testing. The recordings came with no separate open-source license, and importing them does not change their rights boundary.

`Core/FeibiVoiceCatalog.cs` is the official place to maintain the recording mapping. The everyday pool contains the 9 numbered clips plus "normal", "quiet calm mumble" and "Feiba Jiubi", chosen randomly at startup/switching in, Bring Her Home, unmuting, and idle-standing chatter outside Quiet Mode. As confirmed by the user, falling/being tossed and similar weightless responses (`Scream` / `Panic`) choose randomly only among these 9 numbered recordings, sharing the 12-second same-file anti-repeat with everyday chatter; the named emotion recordings are not mixed back in. The original "feibijiubi_falling.wav" is kept as-is but is no longer used by any situation; 24 of the current 25 original files are used for playback. Sleep still uses "sleepy", dragging or slipping still use the aggrieved clips, exploring still uses "puzzled", and the separate short response to a clear landing keeps the original mapping. Hat stashing leans toward whispering and playful soft clips, kicks/punches/charges toward positive/excited, the tear uses "gritting teeth", and endings use the playful clips. Kicks, punches and special moves respond from the wind-up; no extra attack recording plays when approaching the target, so the short approach phase does not use up the interval for the tear's response.

It reuses the existing `.NET 8 WPF MediaPlayer` and `VoicePlaybackService`, adding no audio library, network calls or transcoding dependency; the [WPF multimedia documentation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/desktop/wpf/graphics-multimedia/multimedia-overview) is the official reference for that existing player. The service adds character-folder routing and a mischief-situation entry point, and stays the project maintainer's responsibility. Only one recording plays at a time, keeping priority interruption and the 12-second cross-situation anti-repeat of the same recording for ordinary situations; ordinary mischief has a 10-second same-situation cooldown and at least 3.5 seconds after any voice, and consecutive animation phases may skip sound. The fixed full-bar opening exception is described below. Quiet Mode does not play the new everyday chatter or mischief voices; existing reactions such as sleepiness and user dragging are still controlled by the mute switch. Cancelling the current mischief stops that performance's voice; muting stops sound immediately.

The source and waveform audit is recorded in one place in `assets/characters/feibijiubi/previews/voice-audit.json`, and the review tool is `tools/audit_character_voice.py`. All 25 source/project hashes match, totaling 4,055,284 bytes and 28.136 seconds, all 48 kHz, mono, 24-bit extensible PCM. The binary check found no corruption, total silence, duplicates or near-full-scale clipping; `041` and "dejected hushed" have lower amplitude, and the original loudness variation is kept without automatic normalization. The recordings were not played or uploaded this time; a format check cannot replace a manual listening acceptance.

The opening of the full-bar big mischief always uses `feibijiubi_feiba_jiubi.wav` as specified by the user; the source file and the existing project file both have SHA-256 `D4E687696790B103A73A8710D7EC78ED7BB84FDAA2C130E608974192254D5135`, and it is not re-recorded or transcoded. After a safe target is found and it is confirmed that this big mischief can start, the `BurstCall` phase plays this recording, and only after receiving this player's actual end event does it move on to approaching, throwing the hat and attacking; while waiting, the target is not moved or minimized, and the target is checked again afterwards. This opening is not filtered out by the random pool or the chatter cooldown; it ends the previous chatter line first without overlapping; the existing cooldowns of the everyday pool and the numbered falling pool stay unchanged.

`VoicePlaybackService.PlayBurstCallAsync` reuses the WPF `MediaPlayer` and creates a separate player for the single opening to isolate old voice completion events, closing it when finished, without adding audio dependencies; it is maintained by the project maintainer. When muted, at zero volume or if playback fails, the sound is skipped and the action continues; after cancelling, exiting or switching characters, the old action must not continue; an open/play timeout stops this sound instead of waiting forever. The full-bar opening never overrides the autonomous mischief switch, Quiet Mode or the safe-target limits. The phase logs are `VoiceBurstStarted`, `VoiceBurstFinished` and `PrankBurstCallFinished`; how it sounds and whether she actually "finishes the call before striking" are still accepted by the user.

## Action and geometry contract

At runtime Feibi requires 33 action folders, each with exactly 16 native frames, 528 in total, named `frame_00.png` to `frame_15.png`. Nuonuo still has the original 17 folders and 272 frames. Feibi's folders are:

```text
idle, run, walk, toss, fall, land, curious, sleep, climb, drag, jump, slide, roll
prank_sneak, kick, punch, charge, recoil, hat_open, hat_store, hat_hold,
hat_return, gloat, hat_throw, bare_idle, bare_run, bare_kick, bare_tear,
bare_recover, bare_drag, bare_fall, hat_pickup, hat_wear
```

The first 8 frames of `sleep` are used for lying down and, in reverse, getting up, and the last 8 for breathing while lying down; `toss` uses the first 8 frames.
